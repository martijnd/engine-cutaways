Suzuki SV650 and Honda CBR650R: two engines on one crank angle

Two middleweight engines of about the same size, built in different ways. Suzuki splits 645 cc between two big cylinders set 90° apart. Honda splits 649 cc between four small ones in a row. Both cutaways below turn at the same crank speed, so you can watch the twin's uneven 270°/450° beat beside the four's even 180° rhythm.

Firing events at the same crank speed

Each lane shows one cylinder's four strokes over two crank turns. Diamonds mark firing, the start of each power stroke. Drag across the chart to set the crank angle.

At 6,000 rpm the crank turns 36° per millisecond. The SV650 then fires 7.5 ms after the front cylinder and 12.5 ms after the rear, while the CBR650R fires every 5 ms. So the four fires twice as often, and evenly. The twin's long gap of 450° spans two and a half of the four's intervals with no new firing. Stroke phases switch exactly at the dead centres here, which is idealized; real valve events overlap them.

What is idealized

  • Valve timing and cam profiles. Each lobe is a symmetric bump that lifts a flat bucket, with its peak 105° from TDC. Lift is computed from the drawn lobe. Neither maker publishes timing figures.
  • Rod lengths: 130 mm for the SV650 and 98 mm for the CBR650R. Neither is published. Piston travel at the dead centres is exact; between them it depends on these lengths.
  • Castings: crankcase, block, head and port shapes, coolant jackets, bore pitch (80 mm on the CBR650R), main-bearing layout, valve angles and valve sizes.
  • Direction of rotation: both cranks are drawn turning forward, which is assumed. The 270°/450° gaps and the 180° rhythm don't depend on it.
  • Phases switch at exactly 0°, 180°, 360° and 540° after each cylinder fires.

Left out

  • Cam chains and tensioners, spark plugs and ignition, injectors and throttle bodies, oil pump and galleries, gearbox, clutch, starter and generator.
  • The far intake and exhaust valves of each SV650 cylinder, which are hidden behind the cut.
  • The near crank web of the SV650, cut away to show both rods on the shared pin.

Not to scale with each other

Each drawing is fitted to its own frame, so the two are not a size comparison. Within one drawing, parts keep their proportions: bore, stroke and the piston travel you see share one scale.
